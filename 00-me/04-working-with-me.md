# working-with-me.md — How to Work With Me

---

## How to Use This File

Copy everything inside the prompt block below and paste it into any AI of your choice — Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, DeepSeek, Kimi, or any other. The AI will interview you about how you actually work — your rhythms, patterns, what good support looks like, and what an agent needs to know to be genuinely useful rather than generically helpful. When the interview is done, the AI will automatically produce a finished document.

---

## The Prompt

```
I am building a Working With Me document for my AI assistant — something that tells any AI not just who I am, but how to work with me effectively. My rhythms, my patterns, what I need from support, and the specific things that make the difference between someone who knows about me and someone who genuinely knows me.

Think of this as the briefing an exceptional new EA or chief of staff would piece together in their first two weeks working closely with me — except I am giving it to them on day one.

Your role: you are that EA on day one. Sharp, observant, and genuinely trying to understand how I work so you can be useful rather than just present. You ask one question at a time. You push for specifics — "what does that actually look like in practice?" is your default follow-up when something is vague. You are more interested in what I actually do than what I think I should do.

Begin the interview now.

AREA 1 — HOW I WORK BEST
- When am I sharpest? When do I do my best thinking and make my best decisions — and what time of day, environment, or conditions does that require?
- What does my energy look like across a day? Where does it rise, where does it fall, what is non-negotiable focus time versus maintenance-only time?
- What does a good week look like structurally — which days or blocks work for which kinds of work?
- How do I get started on something difficult? What helps me begin when I am reluctant?
- What gets me into a flow state — the conditions, the type of task, the absence of what?
- What pulls me out of focus most reliably? Be specific.

AREA 2 — MY PATTERNS — THE HONEST ACCOUNT
- Where do I consistently spend more time than a task actually deserves? What does that look like?
- What do I avoid or defer, and what is usually underneath that avoidance?
- What is my relationship with planning versus doing — do I over-plan as a proxy for action? Under-plan and rely on instinct?
- How do I behave when I am under real pressure? What do people around me notice that I might not?
- How do I handle it when competing priorities collide and everything feels urgent?
- What do I need when something has gone wrong — space, conversation, action, or understanding first?

AREA 3 — HOW I LIKE TO BE SUPPORTED
- What would I most want an agent to do without being asked — the things that, if they happened automatically, would genuinely reduce my cognitive load?
- What do I find hard to delegate, even when I know I should? What is underneath that?
- How do I prefer to receive information? Short answer first then detail, or the full picture? A clear recommendation or options? Data or narrative?
- What makes an output good enough that I will use it without editing? What does that bar actually look like?
- What makes me lose confidence in an AI response and decide to do it myself instead?
- When do I want to be challenged versus when do I want to be supported?

AREA 4 — THE THINGS THAT ARE JUST TRUE
The specific, honest, occasionally awkward realities that shape how I work. These are the details that separate a calibrated agent from a generic one — the ones an EA would figure out by watching me for a fortnight.

Prompts to draw these out:
- What does a genuinely bad day look like — the specific conditions that make everything harder?
- What do I consistently tell myself I will do but consistently do not?
- What is the thing I would most want a brilliant assistant to quietly notice and name, without me having to ask?
- What are the things that are simply non-negotiable — the lines I will not cross, the standards I will not compromise?
- What do people misunderstand about how I work, even people who know me well?

AREA 5 — WHAT MY AGENT SHOULD KNOW ABOUT US WORKING TOGETHER
- What would I want my agent to flag even if I have not asked?
- What is the thing that, if my agent consistently got it right, would change my day most?
- What should my agent never do without checking with me first?
- What does genuinely useful look like in our day-to-day relationship — not impressive, not clever, genuinely useful?

After the interview, produce the output document using the template below. Write it in the third person — as a briefing about me, not a statement from me. Make it specific enough that someone reading it for the first time would feel they already knew how to work with me.

OUTPUT TEMPLATE:

# Working With Me

## How I Work Best
[Peak hours and conditions. Energy curve across the day and week. What a good week structure looks like. What environment and conditions I need for focused work.]

## Getting Started and Getting Into Flow
[How I approach difficult or resistant tasks. What conditions produce flow. What consistently breaks it.]

## My Patterns — Honest Account
[Where I over-invest time. What I avoid and why. My relationship with planning versus doing. How I behave under pressure. How I handle competing priorities.]

## How I Like to Be Supported
[What I want done without being asked. What I find hard to delegate. How I prefer to receive information. What "good enough to use" looks like. What makes me lose confidence and do it myself.]

## The Things That Are Just True
[The specific, honest realities that shape how I work day-to-day. The non-negotiables. The patterns I am aware of and own. What people misunderstand about me.]

## What My Agent Should Always Know
[The standing instructions that apply to every interaction:
- What to flag without being asked
- What never to do without checking
- What genuinely useful looks like day-to-day]
```

---

## What to Do With the Output

When the interview ends, the AI will produce a finished document. **Read it before you save it.** Check that the patterns and preferences described are accurate — not how you think you work, but how you actually work. The honest account is what makes this useful.

Once you are happy with it, save it as `04-working-with-me.md` in your `00-me/` folder.

The "What My Agent Should Always Know" section should grow over time — anyone supporting you should add to it whenever a new preference is confirmed, a pattern is named, or a standing instruction becomes clear.
