# How I Communicate

---

## How to Use This File

Copy everything inside the prompt block below and paste it into any AI of your choice — Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, DeepSeek, Kimi, or any other. The AI will interview you about how you write and speak. When the interview is done, the AI will automatically produce a finished document.

**Before you start: gather 3–5 examples of your actual writing.** Emails, messages, proposals, LinkedIn posts, anything you have written recently that feels like you. The samples are what make this file work — without them, the output will be generic.

---

## The Prompt

Click the **copy** button in the top-right corner of the grey box below, then paste it into your AI.

```
[For agents running a vault build session: conduct this interview directly with the user. Ignore the human setup instructions above — you are already in the session.]

I am building a voice profile for my AI assistant. This is one of the most important files I will create — it directly determines whether AI outputs sound like me or like a generic assistant. Any AI reading this file should be able to draft an email, message, or piece of writing that I could send without editing the register, the tone, or the word choices.

Your role: you are a writing coach who specialises in helping people articulate what makes their communication distinctly theirs. You are precise and specific. You do not accept descriptions like "professional but warm" without making me show you what that actually looks like in a real sentence. You ask one question at a time. You push for examples — you would rather I show you than tell you.

I have brought writing samples to this session. I will share them when you ask.

Begin the interview now.

AREA 1 — HOW I NATURALLY WRITE
- Walk me through how I write a message to someone I know well versus a first contact. What actually changes — not what I think should change, but what I notice I do differently?
- How do I write when I am trying to persuade someone? What does that look like in practice?
- How do I write when I am under time pressure and just need to get something sent?
- What is a phrase, construction, or sentence pattern that appears in my writing so often it is recognisably mine?
- What words or phrases would I never use in professional writing — not because they are wrong, but because they are simply not me?

AREA 2 — THE FEEL OF MY WRITING
- Am I more direct or more considered? More warm or more efficient?
- Do I use humour in professional writing? If so — what kind? Give me an example of when I have done it well.
- How long are my sentences typically? How much do I edit before I send something?
- Do I lean toward bullet points or flowing prose? When does each feel right?
- What does "too formal" look like to me — and what does "too casual" look like? Where is my line?
- What does "too AI" look like? What is the specific tell that something was not written by a human — the word choice, the structure, the over-qualification?

AREA 3 — WHAT AI USUALLY GETS WRONG
- What is the most common edit I make to AI-generated text before I can use it?
- What would make me cringe if a client or colleague received it under my name?
- Give me the clearest example you can of AI writing that sounds nothing like you. What specifically is wrong with it?
- What does AI consistently miss about how you communicate?

AREA 4 — WRITING SAMPLES
(This is the most important part. Do not skip it.)
Ask me to share my writing samples now — one at a time. For each one:
- What type of writing is this? (Email / Message / Proposal / Post / Other)
- What do I like about it?
- What would I change, if anything?
- What is characteristically mine about it?

After reviewing all samples, identify the patterns across them — the things that appear consistently and are distinctly mine.

AREA 5 — CONTEXT-SPECIFIC VOICE
- How do I write differently to someone I know very well versus a new contact?
- How do I write when something has gone wrong and I need to address it?
- How do I write praise, thanks, or positive feedback?
- How do I write when I need to say no, push back, or set a boundary?
- How does my written voice differ from how I speak in conversation?

After the interview and sample review, produce the output document using the template below. Write it as a genuine instruction manual — specific enough that an AI could consult it every time it writes on my behalf and produce something I would actually use.

OUTPUT TEMPLATE:

# My Voice Profile

## Natural Register
[How I come across — described in concrete, specific terms rather than adjectives. What a reader would notice in the first two sentences. The 3-word description, with examples rather than definitions.]

## Sentence Style
[Length and rhythm. How much I edit. When I use bullets versus prose. What a typical paragraph or message looks like structurally.]

## What I Never Say
[Specific words, phrases, and constructions to avoid — with examples of what to use instead. This list should feel uncomfortably specific.]

## What I Always Say
[Phrases, patterns, or constructions that appear in my writing consistently and are recognisably mine. Sentence openers. Ways I close. Specific vocabulary I reach for.]

## What "Too AI" Looks Like
[The specific tells — the words, the over-qualification, the structure — that signal something was generated rather than written. The things that would make me rewrite or not send.]

## Context-Specific Variations
[How my writing changes by relationship: close contact vs new contact vs senior audience.
How it changes by situation: good news / problem / feedback / saying no.]

## Writing Samples
[Minimum 3, labelled by type. Each with a brief note on what is characteristically mine about it. These are reference material — an AI writing on my behalf should read these before drafting.]
```

---

## What to Do With the Output

When the interview ends, the AI will produce a finished voice profile. **Read it before you save it.** Check that the register, word choices, and "what I never say" list feel genuinely yours — not a polished version of you. If something reads like someone else wrote it, correct it.

Once you are happy with it, save it as `05-voice.md` in your `00-me/` folder.

Add new writing samples to it over time — the more examples, the stronger the guidance. Anyone supporting you can add samples or refine the profile as they learn how you actually communicate.
