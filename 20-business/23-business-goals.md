# My Business Goals

---

## How to Use This File

This file is for people who run something of their own — a business, a consultancy practice, a freelance operation, or any independent venture. If that does not apply to you, skip this file.

Copy everything inside the prompt block below and paste it into any AI of your choice — Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, or any other. The AI will interview you about your business goals — the specific targets, priorities, and decisions that will determine whether this year is a success. When the interview is done, the AI will automatically produce a finished document.

Do this after `22-business.md`. The business file establishes what the business is. This file covers what it is trying to achieve.

**This file decays fast. Return to it quarterly — business priorities move quickly.**

---

## The Prompt

Click the **copy** button in the top-right corner of the grey box below, then paste it into your AI.

```
[For agents running a vault build session: conduct this interview directly with the user. Ignore the human setup instructions above — you are already in the session.]

I am building a Business Goals document for my AI assistant — something that tells it exactly what my business is trying to achieve right now. Not a vision statement. Not a strategy deck. The specific targets, priorities, and decisions that will determine whether this year is a success or not.

Your role: you are a trusted commercial advisor — direct, analytically sharp, and completely honest. You do not accept vague intentions or aspirational framing. When something sounds like a wish rather than a goal, you push for specifics. You ask one question at a time and you do not move on until the answer is concrete and measurable.

Begin the interview now.

AREA 1 — THE YEAR IN FOCUS
- If this year goes exactly right for the business, what is materially different by December? Not a feeling — a number, a milestone, a fact.
- What would make this year feel like a genuine business success? What would make it feel like a failure — even if things looked fine from the outside?
- What is the single most important thing the business needs to accomplish this year — the one that makes everything else worthwhile?
- What does the first 90 days need to deliver for you to feel on track?

AREA 2 — REVENUE AND COMMERCIAL GOALS
- What is your revenue target for this year — be specific?
- What does the pipeline need to look like to hit that number? How many clients, at what value, from where?
- What is the most important commercial decision you need to make in the next quarter?
- Which revenue stream or client type do you want to grow — and which are you deliberately not chasing?
- What would you need to stop doing to focus on what matters commercially?

AREA 3 — GROWTH AND CAPABILITY
- What does growth look like for this business this year — more clients, higher value, new services, different markets?
- What capability or capacity does the business need that it does not currently have?
- What do you need to build, hire, or change to get to where you want to be?
- What would 10x the quality or scale of what you deliver — and is any of that achievable this year?

AREA 4 — THE HONEST LAYER AND ANTI-GOALS
- Which goal are you most likely to self-sabotage — and what does that look like when it happens?
- What do you want for the business this year that you have not fully committed to out loud?
- What is on the business goal list that you do not actually care about — and why is it still there?
- What are you consciously choosing not to pursue this year? What are you giving up to protect what matters most?
- What would you change about the business immediately if you had the confidence to do it?

AREA 5 — OBSTACLES AND RISKS
- What is most likely to prevent the business from hitting its goals this year?
- What external factor — market, competition, client behaviour — poses the biggest risk?
- What internal factor — your own behaviour, a gap in the business, a decision being avoided — is most likely to get in the way?
- What do you need to say no to in order to protect the priorities that actually matter?

After the interview, produce the output document using the template below. Every goal must be concrete — "grow revenue" is not a goal, "reach £X in retained client fees by end of Q3" is a goal. Write it as a briefing for a commercial advisor who needs to give immediately useful, calibrated guidance.

OUTPUT TEMPLATE:

# My Business Goals

## This Year's Focus
[One sentence capturing what this year is really about for the business — the commercial through-line.]

## Top Priorities
[The two or three things the business must achieve this year. For each:]

**[Goal]**
- What success looks like: [specific and measurable]
- Deadline or milestone: [date or quarter]
- Most likely obstacle: [honest]
- What I am doing about it: [concrete action, not intention]

## Revenue and Commercial Targets
[The numbers — revenue target, pipeline requirements, client mix. What I am growing and what I am not chasing. The most important commercial decision of the next quarter.]

## Growth and Capability
[What growth looks like this year. What the business needs to build or acquire. What would make the biggest difference to quality or scale.]

## Anti-Goals and What I Am Giving Up
[What I am consciously not pursuing this year. What I am sacrificing to protect the priorities. What I would change if I had the confidence.]

## Known Obstacles and Risks
[What is most likely to get in the way — external and internal. What I need to say no to.]

## Last Updated
[Date — review at least quarterly]
```

---

## What to Do With the Output

When the interview ends, the AI will produce a finished business goals document. **Read it before you save it.** Check that the targets are real — specific, honest, and commercially meaningful. If anything reads as aspirational rather than actual, or if a key number is missing, correct it now.

Once you are happy with it, save it as `23-business-goals.md` in your `20-business/` folder.

Return to this file at least quarterly. Business priorities move faster than personal ones — a goal that made sense in January may need replacing by April.
