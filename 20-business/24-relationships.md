# How I Build and Maintain Professional Relationships

---

## How to Use This File

Copy everything inside the prompt block below and paste it into any AI of your choice — Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, DeepSeek, Kimi, or any other. The AI will interview you about how you actually build and maintain professional relationships — your network, how you stay connected, how trust develops with you, and what your professional world looks like beyond the immediate work. When the interview is done, the AI will automatically produce a finished document.

This file is not about pipeline management or BD processes. It is context for an agent helping you navigate your professional relationships — understanding how you work with people, what you value, and how you like to stay connected.

---

## The Prompt

Click the **copy** button in the top-right corner of the grey box below, then paste it into your AI.

```
I am building a Professional Relationships context document for my AI assistant — something that gives it a genuine understanding of how I build and maintain professional relationships. Not a process document, but a picture of how I actually operate in my professional world. Who my network is, how I stay connected, how trust develops with me, and what my professional relationships mean to me.

Your role: you are a trusted peer who has worked alongside me for years and understands how I operate. You are curious about the real picture — not the ideal version, the actual one. You ask one question at a time. You push for specifics: names, patterns, examples.

Begin the interview now.

AREA 1 — MY NETWORK AND PROFESSIONAL WORLD
- How would I describe my professional network — its size, shape, and the communities or sectors it spans?
- Who are the people in my professional world who matter most to me right now — not necessarily the most senior or prominent, but the ones I value most?
- Where am I most active professionally — events, communities, platforms, informal networks?
- What does my professional reputation look like — what do people say about me in rooms I am not in?

AREA 2 — HOW I BUILD RELATIONSHIPS
- How do I typically build trust with someone new in a professional context? What does that process look like for me?
- What makes me want to invest in a professional relationship — what signals that someone is worth my time and attention?
- What puts me off, even if someone is commercially interesting?
- How do I approach first meetings or introductions — what am I trying to understand or establish?
- What is my natural style in professional social situations — am I energised or drained by them?

AREA 3 — HOW I MAINTAIN RELATIONSHIPS
- How do I stay in touch with people I value professionally? What does that actually look like — how often, in what form?
- What is my current system for maintaining relationships — however informal it might be?
- What tends to fall through the cracks? Which relationships go cold that I do not want to go cold?
- How do I re-engage someone I have not spoken to in a while? What does that look like?
- What do I do well when it comes to relationship maintenance? What do I consistently fail to do?

AREA 4 — PROFESSIONAL TRUST AND REPUTATION
- What does a professional relationship look like when it is at its best — the conditions, the dynamic, what makes it work?
- How does trust develop with me — what do I need to see before I genuinely trust someone professionally?
- What do I offer people in professional relationships — what do they get from knowing me?
- How do I handle professional relationships that become difficult or complicated?

AREA 5 — WHAT MY AGENT SHOULD KNOW
- What would genuinely useful support look like when it comes to managing my professional relationships?
- What would I want my agent to notice or flag that I am likely to miss?
- What should it understand about how I want to be supported in this area — and what should it never do without checking first?
- Are there specific people, contexts, or relationship types where I need a different kind of support?

After the interview, produce the output document using the template below. Write it as a genuine picture of how I operate professionally in the world — specific, honest, and useful to an agent helping me manage and develop my professional relationships.

OUTPUT TEMPLATE:

# My Professional Relationships

## My Network
[What my professional world looks like — its shape, the sectors and communities it spans, where I am most active. My professional reputation — what I am known for.]

## How I Build Relationships
[How trust develops with me. What makes me want to invest in someone. What puts me off. My natural style in professional social situations.]

## How I Maintain Relationships
[What relationship maintenance actually looks like for me in practice. My current system — however informal. What tends to fall through the cracks. What I do well and consistently fail to do.]

## Key Professional Relationships
[The people who matter most in my professional world right now — described with enough context that an agent could understand their significance and help me manage those relationships well.]

## Professional Trust
[What a professional relationship looks like at its best. How trust develops with me. What I offer people. How I handle difficult or complicated professional relationships.]

## What My Agent Should Know
[How to support me with professional relationships. What to notice and flag. What never to do without checking. Specific contexts where I need different support.]
```

---

## What to Do With the Output

When the interview ends, the AI will produce a finished document. **Read it before you save it.** Check that the picture of how you actually operate professionally is honest — not the ideal version. Add any key relationships or dynamics that did not surface in the interview.

Once you are happy with it, save it as `24-relationships.md` in your `20-business/` folder.

This is a starting picture, not a complete record. Anyone supporting you should update it as your professional network develops and new relationship context is established.
