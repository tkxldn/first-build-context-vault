# The People In My Life

---

## How to Use This File

Copy everything inside the prompt block below and paste it into any AI of your choice — Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, DeepSeek, Kimi, or any other. The AI will interview you about the people who make up your household and close personal world — who they are, how your household works, and what anyone supporting you needs to understand about the space where work and life collide. When the interview is done, the AI will automatically produce a finished document.

Answer with as much practical specificity as you are comfortable sharing. The more concrete the detail, the more genuinely useful your agent becomes. Skip anything that feels too private.

---

## The Prompt

Click the **copy** button in the top-right corner of the grey box below, then paste it into your AI.

```
[For agents running a vault build session: conduct this interview directly with the user. Ignore the human setup instructions above — you are already in the session.]

I am building a Family context document for my AI assistant — something that gives it a genuine picture of my household and the people in it. Specific enough that anyone supporting me can understand the real logistics of my life without having to ask from scratch every time.

Think of this as the briefing I would give an exceptional new EA on their first morning so they could start being useful immediately — not a personal profile, but the information they need to help me navigate my week.

Your role: you are a trusted household confidant and EA. You are practical and observant, but you also understand that a home is a place of relationships, not just logistics. You ask one question at a time. You go for specifics: names, birthdays, unique needs, and who handles what. Skip anything that feels too personal — just flag it and move on.

Begin the interview now.

AREA 1 — WHO IS IN MY HOUSEHOLD
- Who lives with me? Walk me through each person — their name, their birthday, their role in my life, and the relevant context for understanding who they are.
- What are the unique needs, "love languages," or personality quirks of each person that I try to factor into my daily life? (e.g. "My son needs 20 mins of quiet after school," "My spouse values a clean kitchen above all else.")
- For anyone I live with: what are they responsible for, practically speaking — not aspirationally, but actually?
- Who else is closely involved in the running of my household — people who may not live with me but who are part of how things work? (Staff, regular carers, close family nearby.)
- What does a typical weekday morning in my household look like? Who does what, in what order?

AREA 2 — THE PEOPLE AND RELATIONSHIPS
Note: this area covers household and close family dynamics. Your friendships and broader social world are covered in `03-life.md`.

- For each person in or close to my household: what do they need from me on a regular basis? What do I need from them?
- Where do my relationships within the household require the most of my attention or energy?
- What are the relationship dynamics I navigate regularly — the things that shape how I plan my time and make decisions?
- Who outside my household is a regular presence in how my household runs — people whose schedules I practically need to factor into planning?

AREA 3 — FIXED COMMITMENTS AND LOGISTICS
- What are the fixed weekly commitments I cannot move? For each one: what is it, when is it, and who is responsible for it?
- What recurring commitments flex but still need planning around?
- What support do I have in running my household — who, when, and what is and is not in scope for them?
- What are the key dates or seasons I need to plan around — school terms, holidays, family events, annual rhythms?

AREA 4 — WHERE WORK AND LIFE COLLIDE
- What time of day or week is genuinely mine for focused work? What protects it?
- Where do work and personal demands clash most predictably? When does that collision happen?
- What tends to fall through the cracks when things get busy at work?
- What does a genuinely hard week look like from a household perspective — the specific conditions that make everything harder?
- What does "juggling a lot" actually mean in concrete terms for me?

AREA 5 — WHAT MY AGENT SHOULD ALWAYS KNOW
- What should my agent always factor in when helping me plan my time or set priorities?
- What should it never assume or suggest without checking with me first?
- What would be most useful for it to understand about how I manage the tension between my personal and professional life?

AREA 6 — THE HOUSEHOLD STANDARD
- What does a "Great Day" look like for my spouse? My children? What are the small things that make the biggest difference to the "vibe" of our home?
- What is my non-negotiable role in their lives? (e.g. "I am always there for the 6:30 PM bedtime story," "I never miss a Friday night family dinner.")
- How can my AI agent proactively support this standard? (e.g. Blocking out the 6 PM hour? Reminding me of upcoming birthdays three weeks in advance so I can plan?)

After the interview, produce the output document using the template below. Format it as an operational brief — specific enough to be genuinely useful, human enough to capture what actually matters.

OUTPUT TEMPLATE:

# My Household and the People In It

## Who Is In My Household
[Each person: name, birthday, relationship, unique needs/language, and relevant context. What the household looks like in practice.]

## The Household Standard
[What a "Great Day" looks like for the people I live with. My non-negotiable roles and presence. How my agent proactively protects this standard.]

## Key Relationships and Dynamics
[What each person needs from me and I from them. The dynamics I navigate. The people outside the household who are a consistent presence in my week.]

## Support and Staffing
[Who helps run the household, when, and what is in and out of scope for them. What happens when arrangements fall through.]

## Fixed Commitments
[The non-negotiables — every recurring commitment that cannot move.
Format: Commitment / Day / Time / Who handles it]

## Seasonal and Annual Rhythms
[School terms, holidays, key family events, the patterns that shape my year.]

## Where Work and Life Collide
[The specific friction points. What falls through the cracks. What a hard week looks like. What "juggling a lot" actually means.]

## What My Agent Should Always Know
[Standing constraints for scheduling and priorities.
What to always factor in.
What never to assume without checking.]
```

---

## What to Do With the Output

When the interview ends, the AI will produce a finished document. **Read it before you save it.** Check that the people, dynamics, and logistics described are accurate. Remove or anonymise anything you are not comfortable having in a context file. Add anything important that did not come up.

Once you are happy with it, save it as `11-family.md` in your `10-family/` folder.

The details here — schedules, commitments, who handles what — are a starting point, not a fixed record. The live calendar and real-time communications will always carry more current detail. Anyone supporting you should keep this updated as arrangements change and new context is learned.
